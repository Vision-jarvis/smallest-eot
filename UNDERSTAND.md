# What you must understand to defend this work

The numbers quoted here come from `results/report.md`. Re-read that file before an interview, because it is regenerated by `python -m eval.report`.

## 1. The metric is a policy sweep, not a classifier score
- **Files:** `eval/metrics.py` (`sweep`, `first_crossing`, `operating_points`); parity test in `tests/test_metrics_parity.py`.
- **Mechanics:** eot-bench never asks "is this pause the end?" once. For every knob triple it simulates an agent:
  - **threshold th:** confidence needed to fire.
  - **action_delay d:** minimum silence before acting.
  - **timeout T:** forced end.
- **Hold span:** cut off if the policy fires before the user resumes. The fire time is `max(d, first t with p > th)`, or the hold is simply longer than T.
- **EoT span:** pays `min(fire time, T)` as latency.
- **Operating points:**
  - FCR@300 = min FCR subject to mean latency ≤ 300 ms.
  - Lat@5% = min latency subject to FCR ≤ 5%.
- **Why AUC is diagnostic only:** a model can rank spans well and still be useless if it cannot act early.
- **Hard question: "How do you know your harness is right?"** Our code reproduces LiveKit's published Hindi rows exactly: SmartTurn 42.6/6.5/639, VAP 49.5/7.4/655, LiveKit v1 10.5/2.0/454, v1-mini 27.4/5.4/624, VAD 49.5/7.4/700. It does this from their committed prediction files, and a unit test enforces it.

## 2. The VAD baseline in eot-bench is an oracle; ours is real
- **eot-bench's "VAD baseline":** fires at a fixed delay after the *annotated* silence start, so it has perfect silence timing.
- **`adapters/baselines.py:SileroTimer`:** a real streaming Silero VAD. p = VAD trailing silence / 2 s, so a threshold is a timer.
- **`eval/deployed.py`:** the realistic version. Its silence clock is the VAD's own, not the annotation. With threshold 0 it is exactly the `eou_timeout_ms` / Atoms "release time" behaviour.
- **What the deployed timer curve shows:** 600 ms gives 6.9% FCR at about 580 ms mean latency on eot-bench hi. That is why eot-bench `hi` is "easy" compared with real calls, as the statement says.

## 3. Causality is enforced by construction
- **File:** `eval/harness.py:replay`. Frames are 20 ms of PCM16 and a decision is made every 100 ms.
- **What the detector gets:** only frames up to t, and only partials whose logged `t_arrival <= t`.
- **ASR replay:** `core/asr.py`. Vosk small-hi is fed in 100 ms chunks. Arrival = chunk end + measured decode time. This satisfies honesty rule 2 ("open streaming ASR replayed with logged arrival times").
- **Test:** `tests/test_core.py::test_replay_is_causal_and_rows_follow_spans` uses a spy detector that asserts it never sees the future.
- **Contrast with eot-bench:** eot-bench itself feeds *oracle* words with a 0.5 s lag. We do not.

## 4. Why the model is a hazard over silence duration
- **Files:** `models/pulse_eot_hi.py`, `models/train.py:train_variant`.
- **Training rows:** taken at every 100 ms step of every labelled silence span while it is still silent. Hold spans stop contributing when the user resumes. This is the risk set of a discrete-time survival model, so `P(eot | features, still silent at s)` is a hazard.
- **Duration prior:** hold durations come from eot-bench **English** span statistics (`models/episodes.py`). That gives realistic durations without touching Hindi test data.
- **Alternative:** a plain clip classifier (Smart Turn style), scored once at 200 ms. The hazard can say "not yet" at 200 ms and "now" at 500 ms.

## 5. Why v0 failed and what v0.1 changed (be upfront about this)
- **v0** (`pulse_eot_hi:full`):
  - **Training:** only Hindi Smart Turn clips, all of which are Google Chirp3 TTS (`dataset == chirp3_1`).
  - **Dev:** excellent (Lat@5% 557 ms, AUC 0.98).
  - **eot-bench hi:** no better than a silence timer (Lat@5% about 696 ms). This is a TTS-to-human domain shift. TTS "incomplete" clips are text truncations with TTS prosody, and real hesitations sound different.
- **v0.1** (`pulse_eot_hi`, variant `stacked`):
  - **Smart Turn feature:** adds the frozen Smart Turn v3.2 probability as a feature (Smart Turn was trained on large real-human data).
  - **Fusion data:** the head is fit on Smart Turn's *test* split. That split includes real-human (mostly English) clips, and Smart Turn never trained on it, so its scores are not in-sample (`models/train_stacked.py`).
  - **VAD gating removed:** v0 originally output 0 while the VAD still said "speech". The VAD lags the true offset by 100 to 300 ms, so that gating made FCR@300 impossible. v0.1 scores every hop and sees raw Silero probabilities.
- **Honesty point:** both changes were made **after** seeing v0's eot-bench number. eot-bench was never used to fit weights or knobs, but the *design* iteration saw it once. Say this before they ask.

## 6. What the headline numbers mean (eot-bench hi, 400 turns)
- **Lat@5% and FCR@300:** see the report table. v0.1 is slightly better than our CPU SmartTurn run and the published SmartTurn row on Lat@5%. It is far from LiveKit v1 (454 ms, a cloud model with text and audio).
- **The 3-day bar is NOT met:** FCR@300 < 27.4% and Lat@5% < 624 ms.
- **Why FCR@300 is hard:**
  - 49.5% of holds are longer than 300 ms.
  - To get latency ≤ 300 ms you must fire at about 200 ms on most true ends.
  - At 200 ms the VAD itself is barely sure that speech stopped.
  - Only a strong lexical or semantic signal (verb-final completion, "na?", "ji") can beat it. Our Vosk small-hi transcripts are too noisy for that, which the ablation table shows.

## 7. Dev-frozen knobs vs the oracle sweep
- **Points-matrix numbers:** Lat@5% and FCR@300 are, by eot-bench's definition, the best point on the *test* sweep. That is how every published row is computed, so we report them for comparability.
- **Honesty rule 3:** also asks for a single knob triple frozen on dev. `summary.json["frozen_result"]` is that, computed from `dev_frozen_knobs_lat5`.
- **Interview point:** the gap between the two is a calibration-transfer measurement. Smart Turn test clips are not phone calls.

## 8. Script normalisation for lexical features
- **File:** `core/text.py`. Every lexicon entry exists in Devanagari and Roman, for example `matlab/मतलब`, `toh/तो`, `ki/कि`, `hai/है`.
- **Why not transliteration:** rule-based transliteration collapses short function words. Under consonant skeletons, कि/को/के all become "k", and those words carry opposite information (complementiser = hold, postposition = hold, verb aux = yield).
- **Lexical head:** `models/lexical.py`, a crc32-hashed logistic regression over the last 1 to 3 tokens and their classes. It is trained out-of-fold so the fusion head never sees in-sample lexical scores.

## 9. Budget arithmetic
- **Benchmark:** `eval/bench_cpu.py` on one onnxruntime thread.
- **Per hop cost:** VAD + prosody + lexical + tree head is sub-millisecond.
- **The Smart Turn call:** about 200 ms of CPU (Whisper-tiny int8 over 8 s), made **once per pause**, after about 200 ms of raw non-speech. So p50 per hop is tiny but the max is a spike.
- **Spike fix in production:** run the encoder on a worker thread and deliver its score one hop later, at a cost of +100 ms latency on that hop only. Or use a smaller encoder: the deveshu tinymel is 1.3 MB and ~14 ms.
- **Interview point:** know `ms_per_hop_p95` and `ms_per_hop_p99` from `results/efficiency/`.

## 10. The barge-in arbiter and why its numbers are a proxy
- **Files:** `arbiter/`.
- **Design:** two small GBDT heads, one on the first 300 ms after an energy onset and one on 600 ms. The arbiter commits at 300 ms if confident, otherwise waits until 600 ms.
- **Proxy data (no Hindi two-party audio yet):**
  - backchannel = isolated short IPUs (0.12 to 0.8 s, ≥ 300 ms silence on both sides);
  - barge-in = onsets of IPUs > 1.2 s;
  - noise = MUSAN.
- **What that means:** this measures *acoustic separability of short vs long speech and noise*. It does not measure the lexical "haan" vs "nahi ruko" distinction. The real test needs the IndicVoices overlap set (Bharat-Turn-8k).

## 11. Bharat-Turn-8k label rules
- **Files:** `bharat_turn/labels.py` and its tests.
- **Rules:** IPU merge at 200 ms; hold / shift / backchannel / barge-in exactly as in Section 5.
- **Edge cases to know:**
  - A backchannel inside A's pause keeps it a hold.
  - A long "haan haan haan haan" (> 1 s) is not a backchannel.
  - Shift allows ≤ 1 s overlap.
- **What is still PENDING:** the IndicVoices download is gated, and the channel layout check is not done.

## 13. Likely hard questions
- **"Is this better than Smallest's own default?"**
  - The fair comparison is the deployed timer curve vs v0.1 at dev-frozen knobs on VAD timing (`results/eot_bench_hi/clean/deployed.csv`).
  - The real Pulse `eou_timeout_ms` sweep is PENDING the API key. The adapter, real-time streamer and scorer are written and tested against a local mock.
- **"Why GBDT and not a neural head?"**
  - Hundreds of trees on 30 features cost microseconds, train in seconds on CPU, export to a 100 KB ONNX graph (`models/export_onnx.py`), and handle the missing-feature sentinel natively.
  - The neural part is the encoder, and its fine-tune is the GPU step.
- **"What would you do with the GPU?"** `models/gpu/finetune_smart_turn_hi.yaml`:
  - fine-tune the Whisper-tiny encoder on Hindi Smart Turn plus Bharat-Turn train spans;
  - add 8 kHz μ-law and MUSAN augmentation;
  - early-stop on dev Lat@5%;
  - export int8 and slot it in as `st_prob`.
- **"8 kHz?"** See the `mulaw8k` rows. Train-time 8 kHz augmentation is the `--channel mulaw8k` path in `models/train.py`.
